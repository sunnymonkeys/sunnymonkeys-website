Brandsheet.

How Sunny Monkeys looks and sounds, on one page. Version 3, November 2026: black and white, the sky blue of the mark, and a warm sun.

Logo

The mark is the monkey, always in Monkey blue. The wordmark is Barlow Condensed 700, uppercase, to the right of the mark: black on paper, white on ink.

  • Sunny Monkeys
  • Sunny Monkeys
  • Colors. The mark stays blue on paper and on ink; only the wordmark changes. Black or white is fine for one-color print. Never any other color, never a gradient on the mark.
  • Space. Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the mark.
  • Size. The mark is never smaller than 24px on screen. The favicon is the mark alone.

  • Where sky goes. The mark. Primary buttons. Step numbers and check marks. Finished and active parts of the drawings. Focus and the underline of the current page. The in-house half, in-house plans and the glow in the corner of black bands.
  • Where sun goes. The Build half and the rules above packages. The dots of the marquee. Hover on service rows and table rows. A soft blob behind the hero, opposite the sky one. Never alone on a whole surface.

Type

Two faces from one family. Barlow Condensed carries the headlines, buttons and labels; Barlow carries everything you read.

  • Section headline, 700, uppercaseWhat we do
  • Title, 600A dedicated designer, embedded in your team
  • Lead, Barlow 400Hire us for a project, from brand identity to a working website, or embed a dedicated designer who knows your business like your own team does.
  • Body, Barlow 400, 17pxAt most agencies, a designer handles four or five brands at the same time. They can't know the context of each business, so the work drifts: a different look every month, less consistency, less impact.
  • Small, 15pxEvery package: weekly check-ins, nothing goes live without your approval, and revisions within the agreed scope.
  • Headlines are uppercase, tight, and left-aligned. No single word in a different color or style.
  • No labels above headings. The headline says what the section is.
  • Lines of body text run 60 to 70 characters. Body text is never smaller than 15px; legal fine print never below 13px.

Voice

Plain, direct, specific. We say what you get, what it costs and when it's done.

  • No em dashes. Commas, colons or periods instead.
  • Short sentences, active voice. "Nothing goes live without your approval."
  • Buttons say what happens. "Book a call", "Choose Growth", "Send message".
  • No team size, no invented numbers. Commitments instead of stats until there are real numbers to show.
  • The line that connects everything: "The designer who built your brand stays with it."
  • On AI: "We use AI to move faster. The ideas, the judgment and the art direction are human."

Motion

Motion shows what is happening or what changed. It never runs just to decorate, and it all switches off for people who prefer reduced motion.

  • On load. The headline rises line by line, then the board assembles: tasks drop in through the week and the Friday review closes it. The two blobs behind the hero drift slowly.
  • On scroll. Grids, lists and diagrams come in once, with a short stagger. Icons draw themselves. The timeline fills from left to right. A horizon line draws under the header once the page has moved.
  • Always on. The marquee of what we make. The agency diagram keeps juggling; the in-house pulse keeps beating.
  • On touch. Primary buttons shift their gradient and lift by one pixel. Tiles lift and turn sky; rows warm up. Accordions open smoothly. The mark tilts when you hover the logo.
  • Never. Parallax, auto-playing video, scroll hijacking, sections that fade in one after another with the same effect.

Don'ts

  • No photos of other people's teams. Drawings, or real photos of real work.
  • No dark mode switch. One look: paper with black bands.
  • No third color. Sky and sun are enough. Green, red and purple only ever mean success, error or nothing.
  • No gradient text, no gradient backgrounds on whole sections. Gradients live on buttons, rules, bars and blobs, not on wallpaper.
  • No all-caps labels, no eyebrows, no middle dots, no arrows glued to links.
  • No shadows. Depth comes from paper on ink and from rules.
